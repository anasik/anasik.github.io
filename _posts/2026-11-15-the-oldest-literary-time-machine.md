In June 2018, I wrote about *The Sleeper Awakes*, a novel about a man who falls asleep and doesn't wake for centuries. By then, his fortune has grown so vast that he effectively owns the dystopian world into which he wakes. I remember being intrigued by a passage in which characters compare his predicament to Bellamy's.

The next month, I wrote about *Looking Backward*. Like this one, that post opens with the story of how I first discovered Bellamy.

Nearly eight years later, while reading H. G. Wells's *The Time Machine*, I discovered that it, like any good piece of literature, has spin-offs, or what we might now call fan fiction.

Among them were *On the Surface* by Robert J. Sawyer, *The Time Ships* by Stephen Baxter, and *The Truth About Weena* by David J. Lake. The first and last are short stories; the middle one is supposedly a novel.

I found all three in a Reddit thread about what eventually happened to the Traveller and/or Weena. The theories there are beyond the scope of this post, though maybe I'll link to the thread later.

From those discussions, *The Truth About Weena* emerged as the most impactful of the three, so I set about finding it.

It had appeared in the second volume of an anthology called *Dreaming Down Under*. I found a copy online and started reading.

The plot is mostly beyond the scope of this post, but here's some necessary background: the story is a direct sequel to *The Time Machine*. At one point, the Traveller's friends sit around a table, waiting for him to return and discussing his invention.

They debate the plausibility of his claims, theories of time travel, and the possibility of Rip Van Winkle-like journeys to the future.

I Googled Rip Van Winkle and found another story about a man who falls into an enchanted sleep and wakes in the future.

*Wait, how many of these are there?* I wondered. Google's AI turned up a great many, stretching back to ancient folklore and even political writing.

Islam has one of these stories too. The Quran's 18th chapter, *The Cave* (*Al-Kahf* in Arabic), tells of believers who seek refuge from pagans in a cave. God places them into an enchanted sleep lasting more than 300 years. A dog sleeps beside them, sitting as if keeping watch and scaring away intruders. When they wake, one ventures into the market, where people marvel at the coins he carries.

Christianity has an almost identical story, though it isn't in the Bible. In that version, there are seven sleepers, no dog, and a sleep of about 200 years. An emperor seals them in the cave, which unsuspecting locals eventually open.

I kept digging and learned that Rip Van Winkle isn't even the earliest example in modern Western literature. It was preceded by *L'Ann* and *Aulicus*, a political pamphlet. In Rip Van Winkle's defense, both works reveal that the sleeper was dreaming all along, so neither features actual time travel.

While searching for a copy, another thought struck me: *There's a DC Comics character called Rip Hunter. Could he be named after Rip Van Winkle?* A quick search revealed a tantalizing connection. In the Irving story, Rip Van Winkle wanders into the Kaatskill Mountains, now known as the Catskills in New York. One of the peaks there is called Hunter Mountain. It's a little far-fetched, but exactly the kind of clever detail that might inspire a comic book character's name.
