In June 2018 I wrote about "The sleeper awakes", a book about an insomniac who finally falls asleep only to not wake up for centuries until he's accumulated enough of a fortune to effectively be a kind of the dystopian future world. When I was reading that book, I remember being intrigued by a passage where some characters were comparing his predicament to that of Bellamy's. 

So the next month I wrote about Looking Backward and that blogpost, rather like this one, opens with the exact same description of how i even discovered Bellamy. 

Today, nearly 8 years later, I was reading about "The Time Machine" by HG Wells when i discovered that like any good piece of literature, it has spin-offs, or as we like to call it today, fan-fiction.

I discovered On The Surface by Robert Sawyer, The Time Ships by Stephen Baxter and The Truth About Weena by David J Lake. The first and the last one of these being short stories while the middle one suppsoedly being a novel.

I discovered all 3 names on a reddit thread about what eventually happened to the traveller and/or Weena in the original story. The theories presented on the reddit thread are of course beyond the scope of this post but maybe I will try to link the post there later. 

I deduced from the way people spoke about each of these that The Truth About Weena was the one that was considered the most impactful of the three and so I immediately set about finding and reading it.

I discovered that it had originally been published in the second volume of an anthology known as Dreaming Down Under. I found myself a copy online and started reading The Truth About Weena.

The plot of the story is mostly beyond the scope of this post but here's some necesasry bcckground: it's written as a direct sequel to the events of The Time Machine and so at one point all of the Traveller's friends are seated at a table waiting for him to return engaging in healthy discussion about his invention.

They talk about the plausibility of his claims, the feasibility and theory of time travel in general and about Rip Van Winkle like time travel to the future.

I Googled Rip Van Winkle to discover another short story about a man falling into a long enchanted sleep and waking up in the future. 

*Wait, how many of these are there?* I thought. And so right there I asked Google's ai and turned out there were way too many going all the way back into ancient folklore and even politics.

And now that I think about it, even Islam has one of these. The 18th Chapter of the Quran is literally named "The Cave" (pronounced Kahf in arabic) featuring a story about a small group of believers seeking refuge from the pagans in the titular cave and being put into a long enchanted sleep by god while their dog, also asleep in a sitting position apperead to be wathcing over them inorder to scare away any intruders. When they wake up more than 300 years later, one of them visits the market and people were marvelled by the coins they carried. 

Christianity has an almost identical story but it's not in the bible and differs from the muslim one in a handful of ways: there's no dogs; there's exactly 7 people and they slept for about 200 years and they'd been sealed in the cave by the emperor but it was eventually unsealed by unassuming locals in teh future

I kept digging and discovered that Rip Van Winkle isn't even the first such entry in modern western literature, preceded by L'ann and Aulicus (political pamplhet) but in Rip Van Winkle's defense, both L'ann and Aulicus reveal at the end that the sleeper was dreaming all along and therefore don't feature any actual time travel.

As I found myself searching for a copy to read it for myself, i thought, wait there's a dc comics character called Rip Hunter. Is he also, perhaps named after Rip Van Winke, and so upon conducting a simple search, i discovered that the Rip Van Winkle story features a mountain range called Kaatskill Mountains, said to be the Catskill Mountains in NY and guess what one of the Peaks there is called? Hunter Mountain. While it does seem a little far fetched, it's exactly the kind of creativity you would expect being put into the creation of a comic book character.
